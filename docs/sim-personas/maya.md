# Maya, 19, graphic design student — `search-first-skimmer`

Learner role on the sheet: **adult beginner**.

Grew up on iPads, phones and Chromebooks; recently got a MacBook. Fast,
fearless, visual, impatient with dense text.

## Knows

- Apps, cloud syncing, search bars, tags. Types fast, uses shortcuts,
  guesses when unsure.

## Does not know (hard constraints)

- Any folder hierarchy: files live "inside the app" or are summoned by a
  search bar. The idea of standing in a folder to touch a file in it is
  absent until a screen builds it.
- What a shell, terminal or kernel is; Terminal is "the black hacker app".
- That the mouse cannot place the text cursor in a command line.

## Triggers

- **Skimming.** Reads the first three lines of a paragraph, then jumps to
  the next command block. A warning in the middle of a paragraph is
  missed and the mistake is made.
- **Spatial disorientation.** Forgets which folder she is in; creates a
  file in one folder and looks for it in another.
- **GUI reflexes.** Tries Cmd+Z to undo, tries to select text and press
  Backspace, names files with spaces without quoting.
- **"Why?" fatigue.** Three commands for something that is one click in
  Finder gets a "why would anyone do this" and +1 friction.

## Reading

Reads at most four lines of prose per screen before skipping to the
commands; records which lines were skipped. Bold words and `▶` lines are
read even inside skipped prose.

The +1 reading charge is **not** applied to a screen merely for being long.
It is applied at the later turn where a skipped line causes a rejection or a
wrong turn, and is recorded at that turn with the skipped sentence quoted.
Plain over-length screens are noted in the sheet's density notes (end of
sitting) without friction.
